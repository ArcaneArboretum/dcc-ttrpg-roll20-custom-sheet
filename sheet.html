Time to Floor Collapse
i
This is how long you have left before you die if you don't find a stairwell and get to the next floor. Update this at least once per recap episode, if not more often (when the GM tells you to). Remember not to go down a stairwell too early (no more than 6 hours before floor collapse), or you'll be put into stasis until the official collapse of the floor and wind up behind everyone else that waited to go down, both because they've had longer to grind than you on the current floor and because they'll be exploring the next floor before you.
AI Favor
i
A completely objective measure of how much the AI likes you at the given moment. Spend 1 AI Favor to reroll a roll (after seeing the degree to which it failed, or succeeded if your nasty with it), and use the new result. You can't spend AI Favor to reroll on a Critical Fail, though. Alternatively, spend 1 AI Favor to gain 1 extra non-attack action during the current round of combat (at most once per round). There's no max, and you can never have less than 0. It is usually harder to gain when you already have 1, so don't try to hoard it (unless you want to).
Popularity
i
This is an abstraction of how much the audience likes you. It's one component for getting Sponsors to help you starting on the fourth floor. It goes up or down based on how entertaining you are, whether you do well on talk shows or recap episodes, and so on. It has no immediate mechanical benefit beyond getting some extra, fan-voted lootboxes, but a popular crawler is a successful crawler at the end of the day.
Leaderboard
i
Welcome to the fourth floor! If you end up in the top 10 crawlers of the Dungeon (according to popularity, performance, and my personal judgement), your placement in that list will be shown here. Otherwise, it'll show a big fat Goose Egg (0). This placement updates at the end of every day. Crawlers in the Top 10 find themselves earning extra gold rewards at the end of each floor and have an easier time with sponsors. They're also hunted by their fellow crawlers so that they can claim that juicy bounty. So watch your back...

Health Bar
i
Your Health Bar tells you how much more pain you can withstand before your spirit sheds its mortal coil. When you take damage from something (usually a Mob or a trap), compare the incoming damage to the furthest right segment of your Health Bar. If the value of the damage is greater than or equal to the value of the segment, mark the segment off and subtract the segment value from the damage, then repeat with the next furthest right segment. Continue until the remaining value of the incoming damage is less than the value of your next highest HB slot, or you reach 0% HB, at which point any remaining damage is ignored. If your HB reaches 0%, you start dying (see the Dying Debuff).

DR and Resistances
i
When you're about to take damage, DR steps in like an overprotective mother and reduces that damage for you. Apply DR and Resistances in the following order: first, if you are immune to the incoming damage type, you can safely ignore the damage; then, reduce the incoming damage by your DR total (unless it's Armor Piercing or ignores DR); then, if the damage is of a type you have resistance to, further reduce it by half; finally, if the damage is of a type you have vulnerability to, double it. More complicated math gets involved if the damage has multiple types, so consult the compendium if you still have questions or need an example.

External Buffs
i
External Buffs are buffs that trigger automatically in response to an external effect, like taking damage. For example, Damage Reflection or Resistance to a certain damage type are External Buffs. You can have at most 3 of these effects active at the same time. If you have more, choose which 3 will be active before you leave a saferoom or during a short rest (re-arrange them to the top of this list).
Debuffs
i
Debuffs are negative effects that are currently applied to you. They might come from getting attacked, or be the result of negative bonuses on items. You should write down any Debuffs affecting you here.
Mana
i
Mana is used to cast spells. It recovers when you rest: short rests restore half your mana, long and full rests restore your full mana. It also recovers when you consume certain items, like Mana Potions.

Actions in Combat
i

Generally, you have 2 actions to spend in each round of combat, and you can't hold or delay any actions between rounds. After the surprise actions are taken (if any), normal combat rounds start, which proceed according to the following steps:

1. Mobs Declare Their Actions: You'll see the intended movement, planned attacks, and details about what those attacks do show up in your HUD.

2. Crawler's React: You can choose to use your Interrupt Actions to try to mess with what the Mobs are doing. Evade that incoming attack, or step in the way to tank it for your ally. Anyone allied with you in the fight can use Interrupts in any order, and since time is paused until you lock in your interrupts, you have time to communicate about the exact order you want to do things in before locking anything in.

3. Mob Actions Resolve: Time unpauses and the Mobs follow through with their declared actions, including any adjustments from your Interrupts. If you didn't Evade the attack headed right for your face, it's going to hurt you now. If you did, then it won't. Got it?

4. Crawler's Actions: You can now use any Actions or even more Interrupts if you want, in any order among your party and allied NPCs. You have 2 Actions in a normal round, and any Interrupts you used earlier count against your 2 Actions. So if you did avoid that attack with an Evade, you now have 1 Action left to do something else.

5. Bookkeeping: A quick check at the end of the round to make sure all your Buffs, Debuffs, and other effects are applied. If you're Poisoned, you're going to take that damage now. Your Action counts will reset, and then the next round of combat starts, so long as at least one combatant from each side is still alive.
